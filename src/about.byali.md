About Ali

I’m Ali Saleh, a bioscience student in Melbourne working across ecology, technology, and ethics.
My work explores how systems think and how they can be redesigned to support life rather than extract from it.

ArcUp began as a small bench experiment — tungsten electrodes, a fly-back transformer, and water.
Watching seeds respond to plasma-activated water raised a question that hasn’t left me since:

What if the next breakthrough was built in the open?

That question became the framework you see now.
ArcUp by Ali holds the early form — documenting, verifying, and maintaining the ethical structure that allows others to join safely.
For me, ArcUp is not a project or a lab; it’s a way of practising science that keeps curiosity and responsibility in the same sentence.
Openness over ownership. Curiosity over control. Regeneration over recognition.

A Systems View

ArcUp sits between biology, physics, chemistry, and culture.
Each experiment is a conversation between energy and ecosystems — a way of listening to life through technology.
This systems view treats Earth as an emergent, dynamic network where humans act as participants in ongoing repair.

Participation — You & Us

ArcUp invites two kinds of participation.

You — independent exploration.
Use, adapt, or fork any open protocol.
Attribute your sources, follow published safety guidance, and share your results back to the commons.
Learning in public turns personal curiosity into shared knowledge.

Us — collaboration within ArcUp.
Work through Workspace to co-design experiments, build hardware, and share results.
Contributors inside the framework share authorship, credit, and—when funding allows—resources.
This collective layer forms the foundation for the future Phase 2 community, where ArcUp becomes by many.

Participation is open to students, researchers, engineers, and independent experimenters.
All that’s required is alignment with the ethical commitments of Re:Practise and ArcUp’s safety triage.

Why the Name “ArcUp”

Arc — the plasma bridge, a channel of ionised energy linking matter and air.
It symbolises connection, transformation, and renewal.

Up — Australian colloquial for resilience; to “arc up” means to rise, to face challenge with energy.

Together they form a metaphor and a method:
Arc ↑ = Resilience × Technology (exponential to Science and Knowledge)
— a playful formula for amplification through shared understanding.

The caret ( ^ ) symbol marks the exponential potential of open collaboration — power that grows as knowledge circulates.

Closing

It will evolve through everyone who joins, refines, and expands it.
