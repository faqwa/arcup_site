Ways to take part in the ArcUp field

ArcUp is open by design.
You can engage at the level that fits your time, skills, and curiosity — from observing open research to co-building plasma systems.

1. Get Oriented — learn the foundation

Start by understanding how ArcUp and Re:Practise define responsible research.

Read:

About ArcUp
 — the purpose and structure of the framework.

Safety & Ethics
 — the four-lens model of care.

Re:Practise Framework
 — the ethical base for openness and reproducibility.

Review:

Licensing Guidelines
 — how open hardware and documentation are shared.

Verification Process
 — what qualifies a result for public release.

Knowing the principles first prevents re-work later.
Ten minutes of reading equals days of saved effort.

2. Clarify Your Why — align intent

ArcUp began with reflection: what if discovery were shared from the start? What world would this create?
Before joining, define your own purpose for working in the open.

Note the problems or systems you care about.

Identify the skills you can offer.

Ask how your contribution supports planetary repair.

You can include these notes when you're joining the community.

3. Find Your Entry Point — choose how to contribute
Mode	Description	Action
Independent Research	Use or replicate any open protocol. Attribute, follow safety guidance, and share verified data through Workspace.	Explore Protocols →

Collaborative Projects	Join ongoing builds, co-design experiments, and publish results collectively.	Open Workspace →
 Contributor Form →

Framework Application	Apply the Re:Practise ethics and safety model to your own field of research.	Visit Re:Practise →

Community Network	Discuss ideas, troubleshoot, and share progress with others in real time.	Join Discord →

Learning Media	Watch experiment walkthroughs and open-lab sessions.	View YouTube →

Each route connects to the same commons of repair.
You choose the distance and depth that work for you.

4. Connect — join the active network

When you’re ready:

Submit the Contributor Form to outline your skills and interests.

Enter the Discord to meet researchers and builders.

Follow the Workspace for live updates and new experiments.

Subscribe to YouTube for demonstrations and data reviews.

All collaboration runs under open licensing and transparent authorship.
Every verified contribution is credited in the public record.

5. Begin — start small, stay consistent

Replicate a simple protocol or document an observation.
Upload your data and notes through Workspace.
Use existing templates for safety checks and formatting.
Small, verified contributions accelerate collective learning far more than large unverified ones.
